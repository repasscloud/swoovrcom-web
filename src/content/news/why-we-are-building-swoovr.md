Meeting someone new often starts with a small piece of guesswork. Are they looking for a relationship? A friendship? Someone to explore a city with? Do they mean the same thing by “seeing where it goes” as you do?

Those differences are normal. The problem is when people only discover them after investing time, sharing personal details or navigating an uncomfortable conversation.

Swoovr starts with a simple idea: people should have more room to say what they mean.

## A connection doesn’t have one definition

We’re building for adults with different lives, intentions and boundaries. That includes dating and relationships, friendship, conversation and consensual casual connections. We don’t want to infer someone’s intentions from their age, gender, orientation or appearance.

The product approach is to make intentions useful in discovery. What you welcome matters. What you exclude should matter too. A shared interest shouldn’t quietly cancel out a boundary.

That’s the kind of detail we want to get right before making big promises about the experience.

## More context, less performance

Our design includes a browsable grid of people with useful profile information, rather than making a one-person swipe deck the compulsory way to meet someone. Photos are a personal choice in the product brief; a profile’s substance should come from what someone chooses to share about themselves.

This isn’t a claim that one interface can solve the complexity of meeting people. It’s a set of decisions about the experience we want to create: clearer context, thoughtful choices and room for different kinds of connection.

## Start close to home

We’re starting in Adelaide. A useful community needs people whose locations, intentions and boundaries can actually line up. A national signup count doesn’t tell us whether someone can find a relevant connection nearby.

The first planned beta is a chance to test those details with a supported local group. We want feedback on what makes sense, what feels uncomfortable and what gets in the way.
