“Verified” can sound reassuring without telling you very much. Was an email address confirmed? Was an identity document checked? Was a photograph compared? Those are different processes with different limits.

Our approach at Swoovr is to make the meaning visible.

## Start with the question a check answers

The product plan includes adulthood and identity checks before live discovery and contact. The final methods, provider arrangements and alternatives need to be explained before people are asked to use them.

An account sign-in has a different job: it gives someone access to their account. Signing in through an email or social provider shouldn’t be presented as equivalent to a separate identity process.

Image checks can also provide useful signals, but a repeated photograph isn’t proof of fraud on its own. Context and review matter.

## Explain the data as well as the result

People should understand why information is requested, who handles it and what is retained. Our design preference is to keep the minimum outcome needed, rather than copies of raw identity documents by default.

A private verification process should not turn a legal name or identity document into public profile content. It also needs a route for people who get stuck or receive a result they believe is wrong.

These are implementation and operating questions, not details that can be solved by putting a badge next to a profile.

## Keep expectations proportionate

No identity check guarantees how someone will behave. Verification needs to sit alongside boundaries, privacy controls, reporting and support. It cannot replace those things or a person’s own decision about whether to continue a conversation.

We’ll describe the actual process alongside the experience that uses it. In the meantime, please don’t send identity documents through our website forms.
