A connection app can have a lot of registrations and still feel empty to the person using it. Geography is only one part of the problem. People also need compatible intentions, reciprocal preferences and a reason to return.

That’s why our first planned beta focuses on Adelaide.

## Local usefulness is the question

We want to understand whether a supported group of adults can find people relevant to them, understand each other’s intentions and start conversations they actually welcome.

A broad national campaign could produce more email addresses. It wouldn’t necessarily help us answer that question. It could spread participation across places where there aren’t enough relevant people to make the experience useful.

Starting locally gives us a more practical setting for listening, supporting tests and improving the product.

## A beta needs a clear purpose

Different test waves may focus on different questions: whether profile wording makes sense, whether discovery settings match expectations, or whether a conversation can become a comfortable plan.

Each invitation needs to say what is included, who the test can support and how participants can give feedback or leave. Registering interest tells us you’d like to be considered; it isn’t a paid membership or a reservation for a particular test.

We’re looking for thoughtful participation, not a public queue or a popularity contest.

## Interest elsewhere still helps

If you’re outside Adelaide, your city helps us understand where people are interested. It doesn’t set a launch date for that place. Expansion should follow evidence that the experience is useful and that we can support it responsibly.

The website offers a city selector with major Australian cities and an Other option. We don’t need your exact address to understand where interest is coming from.
